For **US App Store storefront** rollouts. Other storefronts have different requirements. Last checked November 23, 2026.

## 1. Give users a clear choice

**Our recommendation: wherever a button opens web checkout, offer an in-app purchase option on that same screen.** This applies to a paywall, a trial-intro page, or any other page that sends users to buy on the web. An IAP option elsewhere in the flow is not enough for this approach.

**Give both options comparable visual weight.** Use the same font size, similarly sized buttons, readable contrast, and equally accessible placement. Matching font size alone does not make two options equally prominent.

Web can be your default selection in the flow we recommend, provided users can clearly see and choose IAP before leaving the app. This is a design recommendation, not a guarantee of approval.

<Accordion title="What Apple explicitly says—and what is our interpretation">
  - **US external links:** [Guideline 3.1.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#link-to-other-purchase-methods) permits external-purchase calls to action in US storefront apps without a link entitlement. [3.1.3(b)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#multiplatform-services) requires qualifying multiplatform services to also make those items available through IAP.
  - **Visual prominence:** the [December 2025 appeals ruling, page 34](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/12/11/25-2935.pdf#page=34) permits Apple to restrict external-purchase CTAs from being more prominent than its own. That addresses fonts, sizes, quantity, and placement—not just font size.
  - **Same-screen choice:** Apple explicitly requires an IAP option on every purchase screen, with at least equal prominence, in its [Japan payment guidance](https://developer.apple.com/support/payment-options-on-the-app-store-in-japan/). We have not found that same explicit wording in the US guidelines. For US rollouts, the same-screen approach above is Helium’s conservative recommendation, not a quotation of a US rule.
</Accordion>

## 2. Tell App Review, then roll out gradually

**Describe App2Web in Notes for Review and give reviewers a working way to access it.** Production rollout can remain off initially, but the feature must be accessible for review under [Guideline 2.3.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#accurate-metadata). Include the entry point, test account or enablement steps, and how storefront targeting works.

After review, start with a small eligible US audience. Check checkout, return-to-app access, and analytics for 24 hours before expanding. Follow the [rollout guide](/guides/paddle-onboarding-guide#8-roll-out-your-experiment).

<Accordion title="Copy into Notes for Review">
  Adapt the bracketed details to the submitted build.

  > This build includes an external purchase option for users on the United States App Store storefront under Guideline 3.1.1(a). Selecting it opens our web checkout in [Safari / SFSafariViewController], with a return path to the app. External purchase links are not shown in other storefronts. An Apple in-app purchase option is available alongside each web purchase CTA.
  >
  > We plan a gradual production rollout after review. To review both purchase paths in this build: [test account, exact navigation steps, and any enablement instructions].
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How we keep this guidance current">
  This guidance reflects our interpretation of the published rules and what we have seen in practice; it is not legal advice or a guarantee of approval. Apple can enforce these rules differently, and the law is evolving.

  Helium will monitor material changes and proactively reach out if enforcement changes in a way that we think warrants considering alternative flows.
</Accordion>
